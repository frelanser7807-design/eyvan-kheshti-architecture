01 / ORIENTATION

Read the city in two layers.

Tbilisi is best read in two layers. The lower one is the cliff-and-river city: a fourth-century fortress on the ridge, a cluster of bathhouses built over the hot springs that gave the city its name — "Tbili" means warm — and a tight old town of churches, carved wooden balconies and steep cobbled lanes. The upper one is everything the last two decades added across the river: a glass pedestrian bridge, a vast new cathedral, and the kind of confident public space the old city never planned for.

Keep the two readings separate in your head and the city makes sense: the same amphitheatre of hills holds a medieval citadel and a steel-and-glass bow bridge minutes apart. Use this guide as an architectural itinerary rather than a live booking service. Confirm access, opening hours and transport with the relevant venue before departure.

02 / ARCHITECTURE

Six places to study.

The old layers

  1. Narikala Fortress: the fourth-century citadel crowning the ridge — Persian in origin, rebuilt by Arabs, Georgians and Mongols, partially damaged by a nineteenth-century gunpowder explosion. Walk the walls for the city's defining panorama; the cable car from Rike Park rises 680 metres in about two minutes.
  2. Metekhi Church: the thirteenth-century church on the cliff above the Mtkvari, watched over by the statue of King Vakhtang Gorgasali — the legendary fifth-century founder. Study the perch before the building: the site is the composition.
  3. Abanotubani: the sulfur-bath district at the city's root — brick domes and vaulted roofs rising straight from the ground, the most intact of them the blue-tiled Orbeliani bathhouse. Stand back and read the district as one piece of thermal infrastructure.

The new icons

  1. Bridge of Peace: Michele De Lucchi's 2010 bow — 150 metres of steel and glass spanning the Mtkvari to Rike Park, lit at night by LEDs that blink the periodic table in Morse code. Cross it at dusk and read the old town from mid-river.
  2. Sameba (Holy Trinity Cathedral): the cathedral complex on St Elijah hill in Avlabari — one of the largest Orthodox churches built in the modern era. The courtyard at sunset, belltower against the Caucasus foothills, is the composed view.
  3. Gabriadze clock tower: Rezo Gabriadze's leaning assemblage tower by the puppet theatre — tiles, brick and reclaimed fragments stacked like a stage set, with an angel that emerges to ring the bell on the hour. The city's most photographed piece of playful architecture.

The Anchiskhati Basilica (sixth century, the city's oldest church), Sioni Cathedral and the hilltop Chronicle of Georgia memorial belong to the routes, below.

04 / ARCHITECTURE ROUTES

Two ways to explore.

Old Town walking loop

Start at Meidan Square, walk through Abanotubani past the bathhouse domes, follow the Leghvtakhevi gorge to its hidden waterfall, then climb the ridge — on foot or by cable car — to Narikala and the Kartlis Deda statue. Descend through the lanes to Sioni Cathedral and Shardeni Street. Allow three hours unhurried; the ridge section is steep and exposed.

Bridge of Peace to Sameba

Cross the Bridge of Peace from Rike Park at dusk, continue through Europe Square and climb Avlabari to the Sameba Cathedral terrace. The sequence reads the whole city at once — river, ridge and foothills — with the new cathedral as the composed endpoint. Time the terrace for sunset.

05 / PRACTICAL NOTES

Stay, eat, move.

Where to base yourself

The Old Town (Kala) keeps the historic layer on foot but is steep and cobbled; Freedom Square and Rustaveli Avenue put the boulevards and the opera house at your door; Avlabari serves Sameba and the views east. The centre is compact — pick the base that matches the layer you will walk most.

Getting around & the baths

The metro is small and central, the Rike–Narikala cable car climbs the ridge in two minutes, and Bolt rides across the centre cost a few lari — the hills are real, so mix walking with lifts. The sulfur baths take both public-hall and private-room bookings; confirm arrangements on the spot and expect the sulfur smell near Abanotubani. 07 / DAY TOUR · TRAVEL PROGRAMME

One day in Tbilisi, hour by hour.

The morning climbs the ridge; the afternoon crosses the river; the evening returns to the baths. Times are indicative — confirm opening hours and site conditions live before you set out.

  1. 09:00 · Meidan Square & Metekhi — the old crossroads; cross to the Metekhi cliff and read the church's perch above the Mtkvari.
  2. 09:45 · Cable car to Narikala — rise from Rike Park to the fourth-century citadel; walk the walls for the defining panorama.
  3. 10:30 · Kartlis Deda — the Mother of Georgia statue above the fortress: sword for enemies, wine for friends.
  4. 11:15 · Leghvtakhevi gorge — descend through the green ravine to the waterfall hidden behind the baths.
  5. 12:00 · Abanotubani — the sulfur-bath domes: the blue-tiled Orbeliani facade and the brick vaults of the old bathhouses.
  6. 13:30 · Sioni & Shardeni — Sioni Cathedral, the Anchiskhati Basilica (the city's oldest church) and lunch on Shardeni Street.
  7. 15:00 · Gabriadze clock tower — the leaning tile tower; be there on the hour for the angel and the bell.
  8. 16:30 · Bridge of Peace at dusk — cross De Lucchi's glass bow as the LEDs come on; read the old town from mid-river.
  9. Day two · Sameba & the Chronicle — the Holy Trinity Cathedral on St Elijah hill, then the Chronicle of Georgia memorial above the Tbilisi Sea.
