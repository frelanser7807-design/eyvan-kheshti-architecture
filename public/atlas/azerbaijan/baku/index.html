Baku Architecture & Travel Guide

A walled medieval city on the Caspian shore — and, rising on the hill behind it, the most theatrical skyline of the twenty-first century.

01 / ORIENTATION

Read the city in two layers.

Baku rewards being read in two layers. The lower one is the walled city: Icherisheher, a UNESCO-listed enclave of stone lanes, caravanserais and courtyard houses that grew inside twelfth-century walls on the shore of Baku Bay. The upper one is everything the city's two oil ages built around it — the Belle Époque mansions of the first boom, and the fluid white surfaces and flame-shaped towers of the second.

Keep the two readings separate in your head and the city makes sense: the same amphitheatre of hills holds a medieval palace and a parametric landmark ten minutes apart. Use this guide as an architectural itinerary rather than a live booking service. Confirm access, opening hours and transport with the relevant venue before departure.

02 / ARCHITECTURE

Six places to study.

The walled city

  1. Icherisheher (Old City): enter through the Double Gates and read the walled fabric — stone lanes, bathhouses, caravanserais and carved wooden balconies composing a complete medieval town.
  2. Maiden Tower (Qız Qalası): the twelfth-century stone cylinder at the Old City's edge — part watchtower, part enduring mystery. Climb it for the panorama over the bay and the rooftops.
  3. Palace of the Shirvanshahs: the fifteenth-century palace complex at the city's high point — Divankhana, mosque, mausoleum and baths in honey-coloured stone; the finest medieval ensemble in Azerbaijan.

The new icons

  1. Heydar Aliyev Center: Zaha Hadid's 2012 landmark — one continuous surface folding from plaza to roof without a straight line, housing the auditorium and museum. Study the plaza first; the building is the landscape.
  2. Flame Towers: three flame-shaped towers (the tallest 190 metres) on the hill above the bay, their LED facades running fire animations after dark — the second oil age's answer to a city symbol.
  3. Baku Boulevard & Highland Park: the Caspian promenade reads the whole city at once — walk it, then take the funicular up to the Highland Park terrace for the composed view back.

The Carpet Museum — a building shaped as a rolled carpet, on the Boulevard — and the Crescent Bay development belong to the waterfront study, below.

04 / ARCHITECTURE ROUTES

Two ways to explore.

Old City walking loop

Enter through the Double Gates, climb to the Shirvanshahs' Palace first while you are fresh, then descend through the lanes to the Maiden Tower and out along the walls. Allow around two and a half hours for an unhurried loop, more with interiors. The lanes are shaded but uneven — pace accordingly.

Boulevard to Highland Park

Walk the Caspian promenade from the Carpet Museum past the Baku Eye and Little Venice, then ride the funicular to the Highland Park terrace for the bay panorama and the Flame Towers at close range. Time the top of the hill for the hour before sunset.

05 / PRACTICAL NOTES

Stay, eat, move.

Where to base yourself

Inside or beside the Old City walls keeps the medieval layer on foot; Fountain Square and Nizami Street put the oil-boom boulevards at your door; the Boulevard edge serves the waterfront. The centre is compact — pick the base that matches the layer you will walk most.

Getting around & wind

Baku is the City of Winds: the khazri blows off the Caspian and can turn a boulevard walk into an exposed one — carry a layer even in summer. The metro is small and central, buses take the BakıKart, and most sights in this guide are walkable in one arc. Spring and autumn are the walking seasons. 07 / DAY TOUR · TRAVEL PROGRAMME

One day in Baku, hour by hour.

The morning belongs to the walled city; the afternoon to the waterfront; the evening to the hill and the flames. Times are indicative — confirm opening hours and site conditions live before you set out.

  1. 09:00 · Double Gates & the Old City walls — enter Icherisheher through the Qosha Qala gates and walk a stretch of the twelfth-century walls.
  2. 09:30 · Palace of the Shirvanshahs — the fifteenth-century complex at the city's high point: Divankhana, mosque, mausoleum and baths.
  3. 11:00 · Maiden Tower — descend to the tower and climb it for the panorama over the bay and the Old City rooftops.
  4. 12:30 · Old City lanes & caravanserais — lose the main streets on purpose; lunch inside the walls.
  5. 14:00 · Fountain Square & Nizami Street — the oil-boom boulevards: mansions, arcades and the city's Belle Époque layer.
  6. 15:30 · Baku Boulevard — the Caspian promenade: the rolled-carpet Carpet Museum, Little Venice and the Baku Eye.
  7. 17:30 · Funicular to Highland Park — ride up as the light drops; the bay amphitheatre composed from the terrace.
  8. 19:00 · Flame Towers after dark — the LED flame show from Highland Park, then back down to see it reflected from the Boulevard.
  9. Day two · Heydar Aliyev Center — Zaha Hadid's building in depth, plaza to auditorium; pair it with the Ateshgah fire temple excursion.
